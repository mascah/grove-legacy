### Schema 4 work contract

`schema_version: 4` replaces 3; `grove.yaml` at 3 is refused by every
command but `migrate`, with a message naming it. Other record types are
unchanged. Work statuses become `proposed`, `active`, `review`, `accepted`,
`abandoned`, and `done` survives only as the migrated historical claim:

| Status | Means | Fields it requires or forbids |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `proposed`, `active` | not offered for judgment | no `approved*`; a kept `candidate` is the last one offered |
| `review` | a candidate awaits judgment | `candidate`; no `approved*` |
| `accepted` | the owner or policy accepted `candidate` | `candidate`, `approved` = candidate, `approved_by`, `approved_context` |
| `abandoned` | explicit human decision | no `approved*` |
| `done` | schema 3 completion claim kept by migration | as schema 3 allowed; no `approved_by` or `approved_context` |

New work fields:

- `approved_by`: `owner`, or `policy sha256:HEX` naming the `grove.yaml`
  revision a sweep acted under. It replaces parsing the verdict prose for
  authority; the verdict paragraph stays as the owner's words.
- `approved_context`: `sha256:HEX` of the acceptance context (below).

`update` refuses writing `status=done`, and changing a done record's
candidate, so no new record gains the legacy exemption; it may move a done
record elsewhere (reopening). `approve` writes `accepted`; there is no
stored transition into Done.

### Acceptance context

`project.AcceptanceContext(record)` is the SHA-256 of the title, a newline,
and the body with line endings normalized, less (a) the `## Next` section,
from its heading to the next level-2 heading or the end, and (b) each
paragraph Grove appends, recognised by its fixed opening: `Verdict on
candidate `, `Feedback on candidate `, `Reopened with `, `Integrated under `,
`Delivered `, `Migrated to schema 4`. Frontmatter is outside it, so the
acceptance fields never feed their own hash (no cycle). Any other edit to
outcome, scope, acceptance or evidence prose withdraws applicability, which
errs toward "not done" rather than false Done. Code after the candidate is
governed separately, by the existing rule that only the group's records may
change between the candidate and the submitted tip.

### Derived standing (`internal/standing`)

One read operation, `standing.Inspect(ctx, root, records, target)`, returns
for each work record: the recorded status, the effective standing, the
target and the tip examined, the delivering commit, evidence references,
and a wait or unknown reason. Standings:

| Recorded | Evidence at the target tip | Standing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| proposed, active, abandoned | any | the same |
| review, or accepted whose context no longer matches | any | `review` (awaiting judgment; stale acceptance names why) |
| accepted | candidate an ancestor of the tip | `done` (ordinary delivery) |
| accepted | a verified squash commit reachable from the tip | `done` (squash delivery) |
| accepted | verified absent in a complete history | `accepted` (awaiting delivery) |
| accepted | shallow history, missing submitted tip, unreadable target | `unknown` |
| done (legacy) | schema 3 meaning, no new verification | `done (schema 3)` |

Ancestry is answered in one `git rev-list TIP` per target tip, not a
process per record. Squash verification is batched: one `git log --grep`
for descriptor trailers, one `git merge-tree --write-tree --stdin` for
every claimed delivery, and one `git diff-tree --stdin` for what changed
between candidate and submitted tip. Without Git, or without a target,
standing is the recorded status with the reason stated; `list`, `show` and
`check` keep working without Git.

Consumers take this result rather than re-deciding from `Status`: `list`
(a STANDING column; `--status` still filters recorded status), `show`
(`Standing:` on stderr beside `Project:`/`File:`, and `standing` in
`--json`; the Markdown printed stays the file's bytes), `context` (each
selected and listed work names recorded status and standing), `deps`
(delivery and readiness), attempt selection and resolution guards, sweep,
integrate, cleanup, and the board's columns, cards and actions. A
dependency is satisfied by `done` whose delivering commit the execution
base contains, or by a legacy done record under its schema 3 rule;
`unknown` waits.

### Delivery descriptor and local squash delivery

`grove integrate ID [--cleanup]` becomes prepare, retain, squash, advance
and reconcile, each reported as a fact:

1. In the target's clean checkout at HEAD B, find the one branch whose tip
   S holds the record accepted; its group is every record there sharing
   candidate C. Every member must be accepted with `approved` = C and its
   context applicable; C must be an ancestor of S; only the group's record
   files may change between C and S.
2. Retain S before the target moves: `refs/grove/submitted/S` (created,
   never moved). It pins C, the reviews and the acceptance.
3. `git merge-tree --write-tree B S` gives the result tree T or a refusal
   naming the conflict, as today. A policy's `Expect` still binds B, and
   sweep's verification ran on this same merge.
4. `git commit-tree T -p B` makes D, whose message is a Conventional
   Commit, then `git merge --ff-only D` advances the checked-out target,
   refused if HEAD moved. Nothing else is written; there is no done commit.
5. Reconcile: the standing verifier must now report every member `done`
   at D. Rerunning `integrate` after a crash finds the delivery through the
   same verifier and goes straight to cleanup, never a second commit.

D's message: subject `TYPE: TITLE` from the first member's title with its
first letter lowercased; TYPE is the most significant Conventional Commit
type among the candidate's own commits since the merge base (`feat` over
`fix` over `perf`, `refactor`, `docs`, `test`, `build`, `ci`, `chore`),
`!` kept when any is breaking, `chore` when none parses. The body lists the
members, then trailers `Grove-Work: ID` (one per member), `Grove-Candidate:
C` and `Grove-Submitted: S`. D never names itself, so there is no hash
cycle. The trailers only locate: a delivery is verified when D's first
parent merged with S (merge-tree) gives D's tree, C is an ancestor of S,
only group records change from C to S, and the record at S is accepted for
C. A forged or mismatched trailer fails that check and reports unknown or
absent, never Done.

Cleanup deletes the branch only through `git update-ref -d refs/heads/NAME
S` (so a branch that moved past S, a real later edit, is kept) and removes
its clean worktree as today. The retained ref keeps every object through
garbage collection. `just clean-merged` also removes a branch whose tip is
retained under `refs/grove/submitted/` and delivered.

Transport: `refs/grove/*` is outside `refs/heads`, so versions and the
board never read it as a work branch, and a plain clone does not fetch it.
The documented path is `git fetch origin 'refs/grove/*:refs/grove/*'` and
`git push origin 'refs/grove/*'`; a clone without it reports squash
deliveries `unknown` with that command as the recovery.

### Migration

`grove migrate` prints the plan (dry run); `grove migrate --commit` applies
it. It needs a Git checkout with no uncommitted changes under the project,
records `refs/grove/schema-3/BRANCH` at HEAD as the recoverable reference,
writes `schema_version: 4` and each changed record, and commits them in one
commit. IDs, paths, `created` and bodies are kept; each converted record
gets one `Migrated to schema 4 …` paragraph naming what it was.

| Schema 3 | Schema 4 |
| --- | --- |
| proposed, active, abandoned, review without approval | unchanged |
| review with approval | accepted; `approved_by` from the last verdict (policy revision when delegated, else owner); context computed now |
| done with candidate and approval, candidate an ancestor of the target | accepted, same derivation; the verifier then reports done |
| any other done | unchanged: the labelled schema 3 claim |
| anything contradictory, or a done record whose delivery cannot be read | reported; `--commit` refuses until reconciled |

A branch still at schema 3 is refused by versions and the board with a
note naming `grove migrate` there, or a rebase onto the migrated target.
Old commits stay readable with the binary of their time.

## Steps

1. **Schema 4 contract** (`internal/project`, `internal/update`,
   `internal/cli` init): statuses, fields, validation, AcceptanceContext,
   config version with schema 3 accepted only for migrate; update's done
   refusal. Tests: every combination the table above allows and refuses,
   the context's exclusions and inclusions, no cycle.
2. **Migration** (`grove migrate`): classification, dry run, write, commit,
   retained ref, refusal of ambiguity, and the fixture for each row; then
   migrate this repository's records as its own commit.
3. **Standing verifier** (`internal/standing`): fixtures for accepted
   before delivery, ordinary merge, squash, external merge, crash after
   advance, moved target, changed candidate and requirements, feedback
   after delivery, shared group, missing evidence, shallow and fresh
   clones, forged trailers, stale worktree base.
4. **Operations**: approve and feedback on the new statuses; integrate as
   above; sweep approving to `accepted` and integrating through the same
   path; resolve's guards.
5. **Consumers**: deps, context, list/show/JSON, attempt selection and
   facts, board columns/cards/actions, versions' mixed-schema note,
   `just clean-merged`. Keep ownership, environment, exact-source and
   terminal lifecycle tests. Inventory at `556f362` of what assumes a
   stored done after a merge, or approval beside `review`: attempt
   `selectionOf` (prerequisite delivery), `onBranch` and `launch` guards,
   `resolve`'s `inReview`/`doneOn`, `facts.MemberStanding`; sweep `plan`
   (approved waits) and `approve` (approve then integrate); the board's
   status-indexed columns (`statuses`, `placed`, `earliest`, `rewrites`,
   `meta`, `bounded`), review `action` (`a`/`i`), `approval`, attempts
   `standingOf`/`outcome`, deps `treeRows`; handoff `assemble`/`Text`;
   `cli` list `--status`, show `approved_by`, deps; versions history.
6. **Documents and policy**: record model and design, commands, board,
   work and review guides, CLAUDE.md, the Candidate/Approval/Integration
   terms; then a bounded local trial with representative history for the
   owner, and the direct-file versus tool-mediated fixtures.

Each step is committed on its own and leaves `go test ./...` passing.
Acceptance 7's real-agent evaluation and the owner's judgment of history
are handed to the owner; this plan does not claim them.
